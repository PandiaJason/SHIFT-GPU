Dear Editor-in-Chief and Esteemed Members of the Editorial Board,

We are pleased to submit our original research manuscript titled \textbf{``SHIFT-GPU: Shift-Aware, Risk-Calibrated and Physics-Guided Learning for Reliable LLM Inference Hardware Selection''} for consideration as a regular research article in \textit{Sustainable Computing: Informatics and Systems}.

With the massive proliferation of generative artificial intelligence and Large Language Models (LLMs), the carbon footprint, energy consumption, and infrastructure expenditure of continuous autoregressive inference have become central challenges in sustainable computing. To maximize throughput per Watt, modern cloud data centers deploy heterogeneous accelerator clusters comprising diverse GPU architectures across multiple generations (e.g., NVIDIA Hopper, Ada Lovelace, Ampere, Turing, and Volta). However, selecting the optimal GPU for a given LLM and latency Service Level Agreement (SLA) without running exhaustive empirical profiling is an open challenge.

While recent literature (including pioneering articles in \textit{Sustainable Computing: Informatics and Systems} on multi-objective configuration optimization and forecasting-based LLM resource management) has addressed in-domain resource allocation on fixed accelerators, existing predictive models fail dramatically when deployed on previously unseen hardware architectures. Specifically, when an infrastructure operator introduces an unfamiliar accelerator family, standard tree-based or black-box neural predictors suffer severe covariate shift degradation, experiencing a 300\% surge in prediction error and triggering widespread SLA latency violations.

In this work, we propose \textbf{SHIFT-GPU}, a principled computer science framework uniting:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Physics-Guided Inductive Biases:} We introduce \textbf{PICAN} (\textit{Physics-Informed Cross-Attention Network}), which mathematically couples a deterministic microarchitectural memory-bandwidth roofline lower bound ($T_{\text{roof}} = \max(2 N_{\text{params}} / (B_{\text{mem}} \cdot B(s)), 2 N_{\text{params}} / F_{\text{peak}})$) with a bipartite cross-attention representation space ($Q = W_q w_x, K = W_k h_g, V = W_v h_g$) and multiplicative exponential residuals ($\hat{T} = T_{\text{roof}} \cdot \exp(\text{softplus}(r_\theta))$).
    \item \textbf{Covariate-Shift Conformal Risk Calibration:} We introduce an empirical conformal calibration scheme scaled by microarchitectural Mahalanobis distance, constructing risk-calibrated latency bounds to manage operational SLA violations under hardware shift.
    \item \textbf{Regret-Optimal Active Acquisition (ROAA):} We formulate an active few-shot profiling policy that selects informative hardware calibration measurements ($k \in \{1, 3, 5\}$ shots) to directly minimize operational deployment regret near the critical SLA boundary rather than passive statistical variance.
\end{enumerate}

Extensive empirical evaluations across 4,163 real-world production configurations (encompassing 42 open-source LLMs and 8 server-grade GPU types under an automated zero-leakage blinding protocol) confirm that SHIFT-GPU reduces out-of-distribution inter-token latency error from 12.16\% (matched XGBoost rerun) to 7.02\% MdAPE (a 42.3\% relative error reduction), and quantifies the operational trade-offs between latency risk, admission, and power on the evaluated benchmark.

This manuscript represents original research that has not been published elsewhere and is not under consideration by any other journal or conference.
