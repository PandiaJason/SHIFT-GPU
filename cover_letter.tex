Dear Editor-in-Chief and Esteemed Members of the Editorial Board,

We are pleased to submit our original research manuscript titled \textbf{``SHIFT-GPU: Shift-Aware, Risk-Calibrated and Physics-Guided Learning for Reliable LLM Inference Hardware Selection''} for consideration as a regular research article in \textit{Sustainable Computing: Informatics and Systems}.

With the rapid adoption of Large Language Models (LLMs), energy consumption and operational expenditure during continuous autoregressive serving have become central concerns in sustainable computing. Modern cloud clusters mix heterogeneous GPU generations (Volta, Turing, Ampere, Ada Lovelace, and Hopper), yet selecting the power-minimizing accelerator that guarantees Service Level Agreement (SLA) latency compliance on unfamiliar hardware remains challenging without costly empirical grid profiling.

Building directly upon recent sustainable computing literature published in \textit{Sustainable Computing: Informatics and Systems}, we present \textbf{SHIFT-GPU}, a framework uniting:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Physics-Guided Neural Residual Ensemble (PICAN):} Couples a deterministic microarchitectural Roofline lower bound ($T_{\text{roof}}$) with decoupled dual workload-hardware encoders, smooth softplus multiplicative residuals ($\hat{T} = T_{\text{roof}} \cdot \exp(\mathrm{softplus}(r_\theta))$), and 5-seed heteroscedastic + epistemic variance estimation.
    \item \textbf{Covariate-Shift Conformal Risk Calibration:} Uses Mahalanobis hardware discrepancy to adjust conformal latency upper bounds, providing an explicit operational hedge against tail latency under-estimation under hardware shift.
    \item \textbf{Empirical Evaluation of Few-Shot FiLM Calibration:} Benchmarks uniform random, diversity, uncertainty, and SLA-boundary (ROAA) probing strategies for FiLM adaptation on unfamiliar accelerators, providing transparent empirical guidance against na\"ive small-shot updates when zero-shot ensembles already generalize well.
\end{enumerate}

Evaluated across 4,163 real-world production telemetry configurations spanning 42 open-source LLMs (0.12B--27B parameters) and 8 NVIDIA GPU models under a NaN-blinded zero-leakage protocol, a 5-seed SHIFT-GPU ensemble reduces Leave-One-GPU-Out (LOGO) inter-token latency error from 11.98\% (matched XGBoost) and 10.68\% (Roofline-augmented XGBoost) down to \textbf{6.83\% MdAPE} (with single-seed mean 7.22\% $\pm$ 0.75\%), achieves \textbf{6.21\% MdAPE} under 42-fold Leave-One-LLM-Out (LOLO) generalization, and eliminates SLA latency violations across all evaluated thresholds ($T_{\max} \in [20, 40]$~ms, 0 / 125 accepted workloads).

All source code, benchmark tables, and reproduction scripts are publicly available at \url{https://github.com/PandiaJason/SHIFT-GPU}. This manuscript represents original research that has not been published elsewhere and is not under consideration by any other journal or conference.
